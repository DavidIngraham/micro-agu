INSIDE THE SIMULATION

A shared point, with resistance to relative rotation.

The positional constraint holds the attachment together. Effective torsional springs and dampers act between the two bodies.

1 · Spring responds to angle. Damper responds to relative rate.

Prescribed ±15° rotation, one cycle in 6 s. This illustrates the torque law; it is not a free oscillation or a predicted natural frequency. Horizontal motion in the lower schematic encodes relative angle, not separation of the point joint.

Torque on the canopy

The payload receives equal and opposite torques. Spring torque points toward alignment. Damper torque opposes relative motion, including when the bodies pass through alignment.

2 · Yaw stiffness from the suspension geometry

Twisting two separated suspension groups would lift the hanging payload. Gravity then favors the aligned position. This is the bifilar construction behind the geometry-based restoring law.
