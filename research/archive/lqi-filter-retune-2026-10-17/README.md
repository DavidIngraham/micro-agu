# LQI retune with fixed reference filtering

AI-assisted, 2026-10-17. Retunes the exact published controller with `CP3_HRTC=0.7` and existing ±0.5 m/s limits. Only feedback and its matching anti-windup matrix change. The published observer, 10% common trim, reference maps, EKF, original sensing and as-built 132 mm geometry remain fixed. No pitot or canopy truth is fed to control.

The previous backport's fixed compass-bearing gust was not a matched lateral disturbance: CRUISE captured different headings after launch, and paired runs sometimes differed by about 90°. The prior crosswind loss ratio therefore cannot establish a causal filter regression. See `crosswind-diagnosis.json` and `crosswind-diagnosis.png`. The interrupted first batch is retained under `interrupted-earth-fixed/` and excluded. This study defines the gust from the aircraft's right side at onset and holds that earth-frame direction for five seconds. Truth configures the disturbance, never the controller input.

The same correction applies to CRUISE mission weather: the seeded wind direction and thermal center are expressed relative to the aircraft heading/position at disturbance onset, then remain fixed in earth coordinates. AUTO retains earth-fixed weather because its prescribed route is earth-fixed. This correction was made before any mission runs or gain selection. The unchanged filtered gains also participate in a separately frozen training-only selection (`selection-with-filtered.json`); no held-out result is available when that decision is saved.

Five gain alternatives vary payload/canopy pitch-rate penalties, relative pitch motion, altitude tracking, and input effort. Synthesis reconstructs the original published gains as a numerical consistency check and changes only K/AW. Local linear poles are checked; nonlinear SITL screens remain decisive. Selection uses six previously seen cases, including three problematic cases from the earlier study. Twelve new Monte Carlo cases remain held out until selection is frozen. Each selected candidate is compared with the published controller and the accepted local controller on both commands/gusts and AUTO/CRUISE missions.

All five first-pass alternatives failed the 5% energy gate. A second training-only pass (`stage2-protocol.json`) increases common-brake effort costs from 3,000 to 10,000 around the promising tracking gains. One alternative reduces speed-feedback weights while keeping its speed target and reference maps unchanged. An additional gate prevents apparent savings from abandoning the speed task: no paired climb/descent mean true-speed drop >10%, and no mean speed >4.84 m/s (10% above the fixed maximum 4.4 m/s target). The initial symmetric speed-ratio check mistakenly rejected improvement from 3.92 to 4.34 m/s in training case 13; it was corrected before any held-out result, with that selection frozen in `selection-with-speed-task.json`. The original energy, task and operational limits remain unchanged. First-pass decisions are preserved as `stage1-*.json`.

The performance index retains task 65%, motion 20%, actuator use 10%, energy 5%. Release gates additionally require all operational screens, ≥5% median paired score improvement versus published, no median task loss regression >20%, no altitude acquisition-time regression >10%, no modeled energy/power regression >5%, no individual score regression >20%, and no worst-quarter score regression. Mission altitude/cross-track ratios must remain ≤1.2 with a 1 m denominator floor. These finite tests do not establish formal stability margins or flight qualification. The selected tune is not changed after viewing holdouts.

Because the selected speed integrator is slower, three paired ten-minute CRUISE runs (fresh case IDs 101, 105, 109) additionally check delayed common-brake buildup. They retain the 20–100 s weather disturbance followed by prolonged recovery. The release requires no operational failure, ≤5% median power increase and ≤20% late tracking increase with the same 1 m floor. This endurance check was specified before the selected tune's held-out runs; it cannot change the frozen selection.

`protocol.json`, `selection.json`, `acceptance.json`, source snapshots, exact firmware and case-level traces preserve the experiment. `campaign.py` restores all pre-existing source/runtime changes before any intentional promotion. Publication is conditional on the full acceptance checks and browser verification.

## Result

Selected **responsive_softspeed** after two passes and nine gain alternatives. All **168** retained SITL runs completed. The original observer/model and sensing were verified unchanged, the selection predates all held-out results, and the source patch exactly reconstructs the compiled controller sources from public AP base `010ada6a4f0b26910fc18ecca176d2e65c5ed9d7`. Corrected gust incidence matched aircraft lateral direction within 0.10°.

All release performance gates passed. In twelve fresh pairs versus published LQI, median paired performance loss fell 40.9% (all cases improved, range 30.3–45.7%); modeled energy per distance changed +0.31% (worst +2.34%). Command relative pitch-rate peaks fell from about 43/42°/s to 5.8/5.7°/s, and median updraft recovery fell from 21.4 to 12.7 s. Altitude acquisition time was essentially unchanged. Versus the local candidate, loss fell 19.7% and energy fell 5.9%. Individual altitude task losses are not uniformly better; see the full distributions in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).

All twelve selected-tune AUTO/CRUISE missions and all three ten-minute CRUISE runs passed their screens. Final-minute median common brake was 3.8%, true airspeed 4.31 m/s, and modeled shaft power 74.7 W versus 73.6 W published. These are simulation results, not measured endurance or formal stability margins.
